What Golden actually requires

The city's historic preservation program began in 1983 with the designation of the 12th Street Historic District, and there are now three locally designated districts plus a number of individually designated sites:

  • 8th and 9th Street District
  • 12th Street District
  • East Street District

Most exterior alterations to a building within those districts, or to an individually designated site, require a Certificate of Appropriateness reviewed by the Historic Preservation Board. The city also notes that a building over 50 years old might be designated or part of a district, which is worth checking even if you assume you are outside the boundaries.

The part that affects your schedule

The board meets the second Wednesday of each month, and complete applications are generally due at least four to six weeks before the meeting date. Complex projects are expected to involve a conversation with city staff before submission.

So a color change is not a decision you make in May for June. It is a decision you make in roughly February or March. Our timeline planner does the arithmetic from whatever month you want to be finished.

What the application involves

A Certificate of Appropriateness for compatibility, which is the category covering alterations, carries no fee. It does carry requirements:

  • A letter of intent describing the proposed project
  • A site plan to scale
  • Architectural drawings of each affected elevation to scale, including specifications of materials with manufacturers and colors
  • Historical information about the existing structure, including photographs and documentation

That third item is where a painter is genuinely useful rather than incidental. Naming the exact product line, sheen and color for every element, mapped to the elevation, is work we do for our own estimates regardless. Turning it into a submission the board can act on is a short step from there.

When you do not need any of this

Worth stating plainly, because it saves people months: repainting in the existing colors is generally maintenance rather than an alteration. If you are refreshing what is already there, the process is usually far simpler.

It is the change that triggers review. If you are not certain whether your plan counts, ask the city before you ask us, and we will tell you the same thing.

There may be money in it

Federal and state tax laws provide incentives for historic preservation projects that follow the Secretary of the Interior's Standards for Rehabilitation, with applications handled through History Colorado. Whether a repaint qualifies depends on the project, and it is worth asking rather than assuming it does not.

The work itself

Older Golden exteriors tend to share a set of conditions: original wood siding with decades of layered coatings, elaborate trim that has lost its crispness under repeated repainting, window sash with hardened glazing putty, and pre-1978 paint that brings federally required lead-safe work practices.

Preparation on these houses is routinely more than half the labor. A bid that treats a 1890s Victorian like a 2005 build has not looked at the house.

Questions

Historic home painting questions

Which historic districts are in Golden?

Three locally designated districts: the 8th and 9th Street District, the 12th Street District and the East Street District. The program began in 1983 with the 12th Street designation, and there are also individually designated sites throughout the city.

Do I need a Certificate of Appropriateness to repaint?

If the property is in a district or individually designated, most exterior alterations require COA review. Repainting in the existing colors is generally maintenance rather than an alteration; a color change is what typically triggers it.

How long does the approval take?

The Historic Preservation Board meets the second Wednesday of each month and complete applications are generally due 4 to 6 weeks before the meeting. Plan on two to three months from decision to approved start.

Does the application cost anything?

A Certificate of Appropriateness for compatibility, the category covering alterations, carries no fee per the city. The $500 fee applies to demolition applications, which is a different thing entirely.

Will you prepare the submission?

Yes, as part of the project: the letter of intent, elevation drawings specifying materials with manufacturers and colors, and documentation of the existing structure.

My house is old but I do not think it is designated.

Check anyway. The city notes a building over 50 years old might be designated or part of a district, and individually designated sites exist outside the district boundaries.

Are there tax credits for this?

Federal and state preservation tax incentives exist for projects following the Secretary of the Interior's Standards, administered through History Colorado. Whether a given repaint qualifies depends on the project, so it is worth asking.
