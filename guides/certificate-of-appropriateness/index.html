The Certificate of Appropriateness, explained.

What it is, when painting triggers it, what the city asks for and how long it takes. Written for homeowners rather than for architects.

Guide

What it is

A Certificate of Appropriateness is the City of Golden's approval for exterior changes to historic property. Most exterior alterations to a building within an established historic district, or to an individually designated site, require COA review by the Historic Preservation Board.

It is not a building permit and it is not an inspection. It is a design review, asking whether the proposed change is appropriate to the historic character of the building and the district.

Does painting trigger it?

This is the question everyone actually has, and the answer has a clean dividing line.

Repainting in the existing colors is generally maintenance rather than an alteration. You are returning the building to its current appearance, not changing it.

Changing the colors is the thing that typically needs review, because you are altering the exterior appearance of a designated property.

If you are unsure which side your plan falls on, ask city planning staff before you commit to anything. It is a short conversation and it can save you two months.

The three districts

Golden's historic preservation program began in 1983 with the designation of the 12th Street Historic District as a National Register district. The city now has three locally designated districts:

  • 8th and 9th Street District
  • 12th Street District
  • East Street District

There are also individually designated sites throughout the city, and the Local Register includes both contributing and non-contributing structures inside and outside district boundaries. The city notes that a building over 50 years old might be designated or part of a district, which is a good reason to check rather than assume.

What the application requires

For alterations you are filing a Certificate of Appropriateness for Compatibility. The city lists no fee for it. The submission items are:

  1. A letter of intent describing the proposed project.
  2. A site plan to scale.
  3. Architectural drawings of each impacted elevation to scale, including specifications of materials with manufacturers and colors.
  4. Historical information about the existing structure, including photographs and other documentation.

For new structures or facade modifications a context study is also required, covering surrounding structures within a two-block radius or within the district.

Applications are submitted through the city's online permitting system, which requires registering for an account.

The timeline, which is the part that bites

Three facts determine your schedule:

  • The Historic Preservation Board meets the second Wednesday of each month.
  • Complete applications are generally due at least four to six weeks before the desired meeting date.
  • Complex projects require more extensive review and should involve a conversation with city staff before submission.

Work the arithmetic. If you want approval at the July meeting, your complete application needs to be in by roughly late May or early June. Add the time it takes to choose colors properly, which is longer than anyone expects, and a summer repaint is a decision made in late winter.

Our timeline planner does this calculation from whatever month you want to finish.

The design guidelines

The city publishes City of Golden Historic District Design Guidelines, which interpret what the board is looking for in rehabilitations, additions and new construction. If you are planning anything beyond a straightforward repaint, it is worth reading before you design rather than after.

Tax credits

Federal and state tax laws provide incentives for historic preservation projects that follow the Secretary of the Interior's Standards for Rehabilitation. Applications are handled through History Colorado.

Whether a given exterior repaint qualifies depends on the scope of the project and how it is documented. It is worth asking rather than assuming it does not, particularly if the repaint is part of a larger rehabilitation.

How we fit into this

The materials-and-colors specification is the part of the application where a painter is genuinely useful. Naming the exact product line, sheen and color for every element of every elevation is work we do for our own estimates anyway. Turning that into a drawing the board can review is a short step.

We prepare the submission as part of the project. You still own the application and the decisions; we do the documentation.

One caveat worth stating: schedules and requirements change. Everything here reflects what the City of Golden publishes, and you should confirm current details with planning staff before relying on any date. The city's page on historic property preservation and renovation is the authoritative source.

Questions

Common questions

Do I need a Certificate of Appropriateness to paint my house in Golden?

If the property is in one of the three historic districts or is individually designated, most exterior alterations require COA review. Repainting in existing colors is generally maintenance; changing colors is what typically triggers review.

How much does a COA cost in Golden?

A Certificate of Appropriateness for Compatibility, the category covering alterations, carries no fee according to the city. The $500 fee applies to demolition applications, which is a separate category.

How long does COA approval take in Golden?

The Historic Preservation Board meets the second Wednesday of each month and complete applications are generally due at least 4 to 6 weeks before the meeting. Plan on two to three months from decision to approved start.

What do I have to submit?

A letter of intent, a site plan to scale, architectural drawings of each affected elevation to scale including material specifications with manufacturers and colors, and historical documentation of the existing structure with photographs.

Which districts does this apply to?

The 8th and 9th Street District, the 12th Street District and the East Street District, plus individually designated sites elsewhere in the city.

What if my building is old but outside a district?

Check with the city. A building over 50 years old might be designated or part of a district, and individually designated sites exist outside district boundaries.

Are there tax credits for historic painting work?

Federal and state preservation tax incentives exist for projects following the Secretary of the Interior's Standards, administered through History Colorado. Whether a repaint qualifies depends on the project and how it is documented.
