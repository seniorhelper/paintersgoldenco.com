Three districts, a few blocks apart

Golden's preservation program started in 1983 with the 12th Street Historic District, and the city now has three locally designated districts downtown: 8th and 9th Street, 12th Street and East Street, plus individually designated sites scattered beyond them.

If your house is in one of these, a color change on the exterior generally needs board review before work starts. The practical consequence is a two to three month lead time, because the board meets monthly and wants complete applications four to six weeks ahead.

That is not an obstacle so much as a scheduling fact. It only becomes a problem when nobody mentions it until June.

What these houses are made of

  • Original wood siding under a century of layered coatings, where the weakest layer governs whatever goes on top.
  • Elaborate trim that has lost definition under repeated repainting and rewards sanding back rather than another coat.
  • Window sash with hardened glazing putty, needing hand preparation rather than aggressive scraping.
  • Plaster interiors that can key off the lath while looking perfectly sound from the front.
  • Pre-1978 paint throughout, bringing federally required lead-safe work practices.

Preparation on a house like this is routinely more than half the labor. Any bid that does not reflect that is pricing a different job.

Working on a downtown street

Lots are compact and close-set. Containment matters more than equipment, because your neighbor's car and garden are a few feet away. Parking and staging get worked out before the first morning rather than at seven o'clock on day one, and on the blocks near the commercial core that planning is the difference between a smooth week and a difficult one.

If you are not sure whether you are in a district

Ask the city, and ask before you fall in love with a color. A building over 50 years old might be designated or part of a district even if you assumed otherwise. We will tell you the same thing and point you to the same place.

Questions

Downtown and historic districts questions

Do you paint historic homes downtown?

Yes, throughout downtown Golden including the 8th and 9th Street, 12th Street and East Street districts, and we prepare the Certificate of Appropriateness submission as part of the project.

How far ahead do I need to plan?

Two to three months for an exterior color change in a district. The Historic Preservation Board meets the second Wednesday of each month and complete applications are generally due 4 to 6 weeks before.

What if I am just repainting the same colors?

That is generally maintenance rather than an alteration and the process is usually far simpler. It is the change that triggers review.

Is parking a problem on those streets?

It is a planning item rather than a problem. Staging and where the truck sits get agreed before the first morning, which matters more on compact downtown lots than anywhere else in Golden.

Does interior work need approval?

Generally no. The COA process covers exterior alterations. Interior painting and cabinets normally fall outside it.
