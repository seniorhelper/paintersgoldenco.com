The short answer

The City of Golden Historic Preservation Board meets the second Wednesday of each month, and complete Certificate of Appropriateness applications are generally due 4 to 6 weeks before the meeting date. For a homeowner in the 8th and 9th Street, 12th Street or East Street historic district, that means exterior painting involving a color change needs to start its paperwork roughly two to three months before any work begins. This planner works backward from your target month. Bighorn Painting prepares the submission as part of the project. Call 720-912-4676.

Why this catches people out

Someone decides in April that they want their Victorian repainted before summer. They call three painters in May, pick one in June, and then discover the color change needs board review, that the board next meets on the second Wednesday of July, and that the application was due in June.

Now the approval lands in July, the work happens in August, and what was a spring project became a late-summer one. Nobody did anything wrong. The timeline was simply never mentioned.

When review is not needed

Worth saying plainly, because it saves people time: repainting in the same colors is generally maintenance rather than an alteration. If you are refreshing exactly what is there, the process is usually far simpler. It is the change that triggers review.

If you are unsure whether your property is in a district or individually designated, the city planning staff will tell you, and buildings over 50 years old are worth checking even outside the district boundaries.

What we prepare

A COA-Compatibility application has no fee, but it does have requirements. The city asks for a letter of intent, a site plan to scale, architectural drawings of each affected elevation specifying materials including manufacturers and colors, and historical documentation of the existing structure.

The materials-and-colors specification is the part we are genuinely useful for. Naming the exact product line and color on each element, drawn to the elevation, is what we do anyway for our own estimates.

Not sure if you need a COA?

Tell us the address. We will tell you honestly whether review applies, including when the answer is no.

Questions

About the review process

When does the Golden Historic Preservation Board meet?

The second Wednesday of each month. Complete applications are generally due at least 4 to 6 weeks before the meeting date, and the city advises that complex projects should be discussed with staff before submission.

Do I need a Certificate of Appropriateness to paint my house?

If your property is in one of Golden's three historic districts or is individually designated, most exterior alterations require COA review. Repainting in the existing colors is generally maintenance rather than an alteration; a color change is what typically triggers review.

How much does a Certificate of Appropriateness cost?

A COA-Compatibility application, which covers alterations, carries no fee according to the city. A COA-Demolition carries a $500 fee per property, but that does not apply to painting.

Which are Golden's historic districts?

Three locally designated districts: the 8th and 9th Street District, the 12th Street District and the East Street District. There are also individually designated sites throughout the city.

Will you prepare the application for me?

Yes, as part of the project. We put together the letter of intent, the elevation drawings with manufacturer and color specifications, and the documentation of the existing structure.

What if my house is old but not in a district?

Check anyway. The city notes that a building over 50 years old might be designated or part of a district, and individually designated sites exist outside the district boundaries.

Are these dates guaranteed?

No. The planner uses the city's published schedule, and schedules change. Confirm current meeting dates and submission deadlines with city planning staff before committing to anything.
