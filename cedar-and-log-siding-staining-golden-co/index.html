Why stain, not paint, on most mountain wood

Penetrating stains soak into the wood and wear away gradually, so a maintenance coat goes straight on after cleaning. Film-forming paint sits on top; once moisture gets behind it or the sun cracks it, it peels and has to be scraped. On rough cedar and logs that move with the seasons, penetrating products are usually the better long-term choice.

The prep that decides how long it lasts

  1. Clean. A wood cleaner removes dirt, mildew and the gray layer of sun-damaged fibers. New stain over gray wood fails early.
  2. Brighten. A brightener restores the wood’s color and balance after cleaning.
  3. Dry. The wood has to dry out properly before stain goes on.
  4. Sand where needed. Raised grain and fuzzy areas on the sunny walls get knocked down.
  5. Seal the gaps. Checks in logs and open joints are sealed with flexible products made for wood.
  6. Stain. Applied to the manufacturer’s coverage, back-brushed so it works into the surface.

Choosing a tone that lasts at altitude

Pigment protects wood from UV, so very clear or very light stains tend to need attention sooner on sunny walls than mid-tone semi-transparent or semi-solid colors. Golden sits near 5,800 feet and the foothills climb well past 7,000, where UV runs higher still. We sample tones on your own boards, on the sunny side, before you commit.

Maintenance, not restoration

The cheapest stain job is the one done before the wood turns gray. South and west walls usually need attention first. We note on your estimate which elevations we expect to need the earliest maintenance coat.

Already painted cedar?

If a previous owner put film paint over cedar, switching back to stain means removing that paint. Sometimes that is worth it, sometimes a good repaint is the practical answer. We will walk through both with you.

Quick answers

Cedar and log staining questions

How often should cedar siding be stained in Colorado?

It depends on sun exposure and product. Sunny south and west walls usually need a maintenance coat first. We note expected maintenance timing on your estimate.

Can gray cedar be restored?

Usually, yes. A wood cleaner removes the gray, sun-damaged surface, a brightener restores the color, and a penetrating stain goes on once the wood is dry.

Should I paint or stain my log home?

Stain in most cases. Penetrating, breathable stains suit logs better than film paint, which tends to peel as logs move and moisture moves through them.

Do you seal checks in logs?

Yes. Open checks and gaps are sealed with flexible products made for wood before staining.

Do you stain homes in Evergreen and Conifer?

Yes. Our Golden crew stains cedar and log homes throughout the foothills, including Evergreen, Conifer and Idaho Springs.
