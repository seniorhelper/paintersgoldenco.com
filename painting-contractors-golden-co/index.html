What to ask every painting contractor in Golden

Five questions. Ask all of them in writing, ask every bidder, and the gap between quotes usually explains itself.

  1. Can I see your certificate of insurance? Colorado issues no statewide painting license, so this is the document that actually protects you. A contractor who cannot produce it on request is not one to hand a deposit to.
  2. What preparation is in this number, itemized by surface? "Prep as needed" is not a scope. On an older Golden house, preparation is routinely more than half the labor, and vague language here is where change orders come from.
  3. Which product line and sheen, on which surface? Not "premium paint." The manufacturer, the line, the sheen, per element, so you can look up the data sheet yourself.
  4. How many coats, and is primer separate? Two finish coats should be standard, with primer specified wherever substrate is bare or repaired.
  5. How will you handle the historic district review? If your home is in a designated district and the answer is a blank look, that bidder has not understood your project.

On licensing, accurately

Golden requires a contractor license for work within city limits that requires a building permit, at $100 per trade. Painting by itself does not normally require a permit, so most repaints do not trigger the licensing requirement.

We mention it because some contractors imply a city license they do not hold, and others imply one is needed when it is not. If your project includes work that does require a permit, such as structural repair or siding replacement, that changes and we will say so.

Questions

About choosing a contractor

Does Colorado license painting contractors?

No, there is no statewide painting license. Some municipalities have their own requirements. What protects you is current liability insurance, a detailed written scope and a warranty you can read.

Does Golden require a contractor license for painting?

Golden requires a contractor license for work within city limits that requires a building permit, at $100 per trade. Painting alone does not normally require a permit, though associated structural or siding work can.

How many bids should I get?

Two or three, asking each the same written questions. Beyond that you are collecting numbers for jobs that are not the same job.

Why is one bid thousands lower?

Usually the preparation, the product grade, the coat count or the warranty. Occasionally uninsured labor. Put the bids side by side on those four lines and the difference stops being mysterious.

What should I be wary of?

Vague preparation language, unnamed products, a verbal warranty, pressure to sign the same day, and on a historic district property, any bidder who has not heard of a Certificate of Appropriateness.

Are you the cheapest?

No, and we would rather say so. On older Golden houses the preparation is most of the labor, and a quote that looks substantially lower has usually left some of it out.
